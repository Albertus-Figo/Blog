ARTICLE 2

The Frustration of Debugging

Errors are part of becoming a better coder

One of the most frustrating parts of learning to code for me has been debugging. There have been times when I thought I had written everything correctly, only to find that the program would not work because of a single error. Sometimes the mistake is something very small, like a missing symbol or a line of code that does not do what I expected. I can spend a long time looking through the program before finally realizing what caused the problem.

Debugging can be especially difficult when I do not know where the problem starts. It can make a simple task feel much more complicated than it actually is.

Over time, I learned that changing random parts of the code usually does not help. Instead, I try to go through the program step by step and check what each part is doing. This makes it easier to find where the problem begins and understand why the error happened.

The biggest thing debugging has taught me is to be more patient with my mistakes. Every error and debugging experience gives me a chance to understand something that I did not understand before, and fixing the error can sometimes show me a new perspective of how I can code better. Even as an advance coder, I still frequently experience many errors in my code. So to beginners who are still learning to code, my advice is to look at errors as helpful feedback rather than failures, and to take your time whenever you're in the process of debugging, it's a process that every coder needs to experience before they can become an expert.
